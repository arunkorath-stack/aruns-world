Questions

Do students really earn marks from playing?
Yes, in the course view. Each model has a benchmark score and a share of the course's ten marks: 2, 2.5 and 4 for the three games, and 0.5 for each reading check. Only your best run counts.
What does a student need to play?
A browser. It runs on a laptop with the keyboard or on a phone by tapping where to walk. Nothing to install and nothing to download beyond this page.
How long is a level?
A round is one to two and a half minutes. With the reading and a few retries, a level takes a student about 15 to 20 minutes.
Where are scores and reflections kept?
In this version, only in the student's own browser. A class version would send them to a course server so an instructor can see the marks.
Is there a multiplayer mode?
Not yet. Level 4, Equilibrium, brings other traders to the island; until then you compete against computer traders on the leaderboard.
Can an instructor change the numbers?
Yes. Prices, timings and benchmarks are plain settings at the top of the game code, so a course can be tuned to its own students.
Why a game rather than a textbook?
Because the idea arrives as an experience first. A student who has just watched their own coconuts sell for less and less already knows what a demand curve is; the page only has to name it.
